\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What transferred}
The cross-fitted simplex stack is a sound default on these tasks. It never lost to an equal-weight ensemble, it improved calibration on both classification tasks, and it was the best probabilistic classifier on glass. On the other tasks it was statistically indistinguishable from the best single expert, which is itself selected by inner CV.
The novelty-damped local OOF correction, the part that SIG-Net confirmed for chemical properties, transferred only partly.
It produced a consistent but small gain on the experimental band gap: all five folds improved, by 0.7\% of the MAE, and the gain survives Holm correction.
It made no measurable difference on steels.
On the two classification tasks its own inner-CV selection almost never switched it on.
We read the band-gap result as a genuine but modest effect. It is of the size that the honesty rules of this study led us to expect, and much smaller than what is needed to close the gaps to the leaderboard leaders.

\paragraph{Why it does not do more}
The support-stratified analysis points to a mechanism.
On both regression tasks the naive correction is harmful for test compositions that have a near-duplicate in the training set. This is where the deployed stack has already absorbed the neighbour's error, and adding the neighbour's OOF residual double-counts it.
The correction helps only at intermediate and large distances.
The fold-matched variant was designed to remove exactly this absorption term, but it did not help in practice. One possible reason: the pair-excluded refits are trained on 60\% of the fold, and on these data that appears to make the transferred analogue estimates noisier than the bias they remove.
We regard this as a negative result for that proposal, not a refutation of the decomposition, which is an algebraic identity.
The label-permutation control also shows that selecting a non-zero correction strength is weak evidence of local structure, because shrinkage toward a local mean pays off even against permuted labels.
A stricter selection rule, for example a one-standard-error rule over inner folds, might switch the correction off more often. It would not have changed the qualitative conclusions here.

\paragraph{The leaderboard}
With CPU-only, off-the-shelf experts and fully nested selection, the method would rank \SteelsRank{}, \GapRank{}, \MetalRank{} and \GlassRank{} on steels, expt\_gap, expt\_is\_metal and glass among the leaderboard entries of 18 October 2026. It is never first.
The gaps to Darwin on expt\_gap and to MODNet v0.1.12 on glass are far larger than anything a residual correction contributed here. Closing them would need better base representations, not a better correction stage.
For the classification tasks we found that most board entries recorded hard labels, so their ``ROC-AUC'' is a balanced accuracy. Comparing probability ROC-AUCs with these values would have inflated our standing considerably, for example \MetalAUCprob{} versus 0.9598 on expt\_is\_metal.
We recommend that future Matbench classification submissions report both quantities explicitly.

\paragraph{Limitations}
The study covers four composition tasks only. The expert pool is restricted to CPU-feasible models (no CrabNet, Roost, MODNet or TabPFN experts), and five official folds give little power for per-task comparisons; the Nadeau--Bengio $p$-values in Table~\ref{tab:tests} show how weak fold-level evidence is.
The set of candidate design configurations was itself constructed while looking at inner-CV results on fold~0's training rows; only the choice among the candidates was made inside every training fold.
We did not tune the expert hyperparameters per fold beyond the internal kernel-ridge grid, and we used a single seed.
The fold-matched arm was implemented in a simplified form of the proposal: the same damping family as the naive correction, two composition distances, and no element mover's distance.
The ideation, prior-art and review steps that suggested it were partly automated, and their full records are in the repository.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
Under a pre-registered, fully nested protocol on the official Matbench folds, cross-fitted stacking of diverse composition experts is a reliable baseline. The novelty-damped local OOF residual correction transfers only weakly from chemistry to materials: a 0.7\% MAE gain on the experimental band gap, and none elsewhere.
The correction hurts for near-duplicate compositions. A fold-matched variant aimed at that failure did not fix it.
We report these mixed results in full, together with every arm, fold and selection, as a calibrated data point for anyone tempted to bolt a residual-correction stage onto a materials model.
