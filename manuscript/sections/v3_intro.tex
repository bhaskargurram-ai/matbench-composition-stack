\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Experimental materials properties are expensive to measure, so the datasets are small. Yet for many compounds a cheap computed estimate of a closely related property exists.
The experimental band gap is the canonical example. Density-functional theory (DFT) with semi-local functionals underestimates gaps systematically, but the computed gap still carries much of the chemical trend. Large DFT databases such as the Materials Project \citep{jain2013mp} cover tens of thousands of compositions.
Using computed values as inputs or as a low-fidelity source has a long history.
\citet{lee2016gap} used Kohn--Sham gaps (PBE and mBJ) together with elemental and compound predictors to predict $G_0W_0$ gaps of 270 inorganic compounds. The test RMSE fell from 0.59~eV, for a linear model on the Kohn--Sham gap alone, to 0.24~eV for support-vector regression on the full predictor set.
\citet{chen2021multifidelity} trained multi-fidelity graph networks on PBE and higher-fidelity gaps. Including the low-fidelity PBE gaps reduced the MAE of experimental band-gap prediction by 22--45\%. Their model, however, needs a crystal structure for every compound.
Related approaches include multi-fidelity co-kriging \citep{pilania2017mf}, stacked transfer from DFT-trained models \citep{kauwe2020bandgap}, deep transfer learning \citep{jha2019transfer}, multi-fidelity denoising \citep{liu2022denoise} and knowledge transfer for experimental gaps \citep{ko2024gap}.
Our DFT block is the simplest member of this family: a lookup of PBE quantities by reduced formula, used as features. It needs no structure for the target compound, which the composition-only Matbench tasks do not provide.
On the public Matbench v0.1 benchmark \citep{dunn2020matbench}, the composition-only experimental tasks are led, among the merged entries, by models that use no external computed property data \citep{matbenchboard}. An AutoML pipeline (TPOT-Mat) leads on steels and MODNet \citep{debreuck2021modnet} on glass-forming ability; both use only the formula and the task's training labels. Darwin leads on the two band-gap-derived tasks. It fine-tunes the DARWIN natural-science large language model \citep{xie2023master,xie2023darwin}, so it brings external knowledge from pre-training on scientific text, whose overlap with reported experimental values is unknown.

This paper asks how far a DFT-informed but otherwise conventional pipeline gets on these four tasks under a strict protocol. The pipeline is a cross-fitted stack of composition experts \citep{wolpert1992stacked,breiman1996stacked,vanderlaan2007super}. The protocol has four rules:
\begin{itemize}
\item only the official folds are used;
\item every model, hyperparameter and design choice is made by cross-validation inside each training fold;
\item the analysis plan is written, and the code frozen by SHA-256 hash, before any test fold is scored;
\item every task, fold and comparison is reported.
\end{itemize}

We report two pre-registered runs.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{First run.} It tested the transfer of a novelty-damped local out-of-fold residual correction from our earlier chemistry study \citep{gurram2026signet}. The correction transferred only weakly, and we report this as a short negative finding.
\item \textbf{Second run.} Written after the first run's results were seen, and disclosed as such, it added a DFT feature block, a hurdle model for the zero-inflated band gap (cf.\ the mixed-variable view of \citealp{ma2025gap}), further descriptors and tuned gradient boosting.
\end{itemize}

Against the leaders, the DFT-informed pipeline:
\begin{itemize}
\item lowers the experimental band-gap MAE below the best merged leaderboard entry;
\item is on par for metallicity and steel yield strength;
\item remains clearly behind on glass formation.
\end{itemize}
Our pipeline uses external computed data, and the expt\_gap leader uses external knowledge of a different kind (language-model pre-training), so beating it is not a like-for-like comparison of learning algorithms. It shows what disclosed transfer of computed data adds under a fully nested protocol.
A post-hoc ablation, labelled as such, quantifies how much of the gain is due to the DFT features (Section~\ref{sec:ablation}):
\begin{itemize}
\item without them, \AblNoDftGapShareOfLeaderGain\% of the band-gap margin over the leader disappears, and the pipeline is on par with the leader rather than ahead of it;
\item without them, the metallicity result falls significantly below the leader.
\end{itemize}
